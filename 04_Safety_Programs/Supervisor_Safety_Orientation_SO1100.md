# 6. Who Is a Supervisor?

The OHSA defines a supervisor as a person who:

> **has charge of a workplace or authority over a worker.**

Mike's Mechanical Services role clearly includes authority over workers.

Therefore the supervisor safety framework applies to him.

The important point is functional authority, not merely job title.

---

# 7. Supervisor Identity Changes the Duty

A trades employee may be responsible for performing work safely.

A supervisor has an additional responsibility:

> **to organize, direct, monitor, and control work so workers are protected.**

Mike's safety role is therefore different from simply being an experienced mechanical worker.

# 9. Shared Responsibility Does Not Mean Equal Responsibility

Authority changes responsibility.

# 12. Supervisor Competence

A supervisor must be competent for the work being supervised.

Competence includes appropriate:

- Knowledge.
- Training.
- Experience.
- Familiarity with applicable legislation.
- Knowledge of actual and potential hazards.

# 23. Site/Task-Specific Training

Waterloo states that supervisors are responsible for conducting site/task-specific practical training.

The current Safety Office sequence includes:

1. Confirm mandatory and hazard-specific training.
2. Have the worker review relevant SOPs, risk assessments, job-hazard analyses, and operating manuals.
3. Demonstrate the task.
4. Have the worker observe.
5. Have the worker perform the task under observation.
6. Verify safety performance.
7. Document the training.

This is a powerful competency model for Mechanical Services.

---

# 24. Read → Watch → Perform → Verify

For practical Mechanical Services training, Claude should use the following project shorthand:

> **READ → WATCH → PERFORM → VERIFY**

### READ

Worker reviews:

- SOP.
- Risk assessment.
- Manufacturer instructions.
- Relevant work instructions.

### WATCH

Competent trainer demonstrates the work.

### PERFORM

Worker performs the task under supervision.

### VERIFY

Supervisor or competent trainer verifies safe performance.

Only then should independent work be considered where appropriate.

# 65. Final SO1100 Statement

Supervisor Safety Orientation changes the basic safety question from:

> **Does the worker know how to do the job?**

to:

> **Have I, as the supervisor, done what is required to ensure this worker can perform this particular job safely?**

For Mike, that means asking:

> **What training do I need as the supervisor?**

> **What training does this worker need?**

> **Is the training current?**

> **Does the worker understand the actual equipment and site?**

> **Have I seen them perform the task safely?**

> **What changed since they were trained?**

> **Is closer supervision required?**

> **Can I prove the training occurred?**

> **Should this person be assigned this work today?**

SO1100 establishes awareness.

Good supervision turns awareness into:

> **competence,**

> **verification,**

> **coaching,**

> **accountability,**

> **and safe assignment.**

That is supervisor training becoming architecture.

That is the SO1100 framework for the Mechanical Services Supervisor Intelligence System.
