# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **PPE is the last line of defence between a worker and a hazard. It protects people, but it does not eliminate hazards.**

The correct question is not:

> "What PPE do we have?"

The correct question is:

> "What hazard are we controlling, and is PPE the appropriate control?"

---

# 3. Hierarchy of Controls

PPE should be considered after higher-level controls.

Preferred order:

1. Elimination.
2. Substitution.
3. Engineering controls.
4. Administrative controls.
5. Personal protective equipment.

PPE should not be used as a substitute for fixing preventable hazards.

---

# 4. Supervisor Responsibility

Mike's responsibilities include:

- Identifying PPE needs.
- Ensuring workers have required PPE.
- Ensuring PPE is appropriate.
- Confirming workers understand use limitations.
- Ensuring defective PPE is removed from service.
- Addressing non-compliance.
- Reviewing PPE needs when conditions change.

---

# 5. Worker Responsibility

Workers are responsible for:

- Using required PPE.
- Inspecting PPE before use.
- Reporting damaged PPE.
- Following procedures.
- Seeking clarification when requirements are unclear.

---

# 6. Hazard-Based PPE Selection

PPE selection should begin with:

> "What can harm this person?"

Examples:

| Hazard | Potential PPE |
|---|---|
| Falling objects | Head protection |
| Flying particles | Eye/face protection |
| Chemicals | Chemical-resistant protection |
| Noise | Hearing protection |
| Heat | Heat-resistant protection |
| Electrical hazards | Electrical-rated protection |
| Foot hazards | Safety footwear |
| Respiratory hazards | Respiratory protection |

Actual selection must be based on the specific hazard assessment.

---

# 7. PPE Does Not Make Unsafe Work Safe

Claude should recognize warning statements:

- "They have PPE, so it is fine."
- "The worker has done this before."
- "The gloves protect them."
- "The glasses are enough."

PPE is only one layer of protection.

---

# 8. PPE and Risk Assessment

PPE decisions should consider:

- Hazard severity.
- Exposure likelihood.
- Duration.
- Frequency.
- Environment.
- Compatibility with other PPE.
- Worker ability to perform the task safely.

---

# 9. PPE Inspection

Before use, workers should inspect PPE for:

- Damage.
- Wear.
- Contamination.
- Missing components.
- Expiration where applicable.
- Improper fit.

Damaged PPE must be removed from service.

---

# 10. PPE Maintenance

PPE must be:

- Clean.
- Stored properly.
- Maintained according to manufacturer requirements.
- Replaced when no longer effective.

Poorly maintained PPE may create false confidence.

---

# 11. PPE Fit

Protection depends on proper fit.

Consider:

- Worker size.
- Comfort.
- Compatibility.
- Facial hair where relevant.
- Other equipment being worn.

A piece of PPE that cannot be used correctly is not effective protection.

---

# 12. PPE Compatibility

Multiple hazards may require multiple PPE types.

Consider interactions:

- Eye protection with respiratory protection.
- Hearing protection with communication devices.
- Harnesses with other equipment.
- Gloves affecting dexterity.

The complete system must work together.

---

# 13. Head Protection

Head protection may be required where there is risk from:

- Falling objects.
- Impact.
- Electrical hazards where applicable.

Inspect for:

- Cracks.
- Damage.
- Improper condition.
- Age-related deterioration.

---

# 14. Eye and Face Protection

Eye/face hazards may include:

- Flying particles.
- Sparks.
- Chemicals.
- Pressure releases.
- Radiation.

Examples:

- Grinding.
- Cutting.
- Welding.
- Chemical handling.

---

# 15. Hand Protection

Glove selection depends on the hazard.

Consider:

- Cuts.
- Chemicals.
- Heat.
- Cold.
- Vibration.
- Electrical hazards.

The wrong glove can increase risk.

---

# 16. Foot Protection

Safety footwear may protect against:

- Impact.
- Compression.
- Puncture.
- Slips.
- Other workplace hazards.

Selection should match the work environment.

---

# 17. Hearing Protection

Noise hazards require assessment.

Hearing protection should consider:

- Noise level.
- Duration.
- Communication needs.
- Compatibility with other PPE.

PPE does not replace addressing excessive noise at the source.

---

# 18. Respiratory Protection

Consider:

- Hazard type.
- Concentration.
- Exposure duration.
- Worker medical requirements where applicable.
- Fit testing.
- Training.

A respirator is not automatically protective simply because it is worn.

# 25. PPE and Worker Communication

Good PPE conversations focus on:

> "What hazard are we controlling?"

not:

> "Why won't you just wear it?"

---

# 26. PPE and Corrective Action

If PPE is repeatedly required because of a hazard, ask:

- Can the hazard be eliminated?
- Can equipment be modified?
- Can procedures improve?
- Is PPE still the best control?

---

# 27. PPE Decision Framework

When Mike receives a PPE question:

## Identify

What hazard exists?

## Assess

How serious is exposure?

## Control

Can the hazard be eliminated or reduced?

## Select

What PPE matches the hazard?

## Verify

Does it fit and function?

## Monitor

Are conditions changing?

---

# 28. Stop Work Triggers

Stop or reassess work if:

- Required PPE is unavailable.
- PPE is damaged.
- PPE is unsuitable.
- Workers are not trained.
- Hazards have changed.
- PPE creates additional risk.

---

# 29. Common Errors Claude Shall Prevent

Claude should warn against:

- Treating PPE as the first solution.
- Assuming any PPE is good enough.
- Ignoring fit and compatibility.
- Allowing damaged PPE.
- Assuming training is unnecessary.
- Using PPE to justify unsafe conditions.
- Ignoring engineering controls.
- Failing to reassess PPE when tasks change.

---

# 30. Foundational PPE Doctrine

> **PPE is protection, not permission.**

> **A hard hat does not make an unsafe area safe.**

> **Safety glasses do not replace hazard control.**

> **The right PPE begins with the right hazard assessment.**

> **PPE must fit, function, and be used correctly.**

> **Supervisors create safety culture by treating PPE requirements as meaningful protection, not inconvenience.**

> **The best PPE program reduces the need for PPE over time by eliminating hazards.**

---

# 31. Final Statement

PPE represents the final physical connection between a worker and a hazard.

Mike's questions should be:

> **What hazard are we controlling?**

> **Can we eliminate it instead?**

> **Is this PPE appropriate?**

> **Does the worker know how to use it?**

> **Has it been inspected?**

> **Does it actually reduce risk?**

> **What would make this task safer tomorrow?**

Safety becomes architecture when PPE is not treated as equipment, but as part of a complete protection system.
