# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **A good inspection does not search for someone to blame. It searches for hazards before someone gets hurt.**

Inspections are a proactive safety control.

---

# 3. Purpose of Inspections

Workplace inspections help identify:

- Unsafe conditions.
- Unsafe practices.
- Equipment issues.
- Housekeeping concerns.
- Procedure gaps.
- Training needs.
- Emerging hazards.

The goal is prevention.

---

# 4. Supervisor Responsibility

Mike's responsibilities include:

- Regularly observing workplace conditions.
- Identifying hazards.
- Ensuring corrective actions occur.
- Following up on unresolved issues.
- Communicating expectations.
- Escalating significant risks.

---

# 5. Inspection Mindset

The question is not:

> "What did someone do wrong?"

The question is:

> "What could cause harm, and how do we prevent it?"

---

# 6. What Should Be Inspected?

Mechanical Services inspections may include:

- Mechanical rooms.
- Equipment spaces.
- Shops.
- Work areas.
- Storage areas.
- Access routes.
- Emergency equipment.
- Tools.
- PPE practices.
- Lockout practices.
- Contractor activities.

---

# 7. Hazard Categories

Look for:

## Physical Hazards

- Trips.
- Falls.
- Moving equipment.
- Poor access.
- Damaged structures.

## Energy Hazards

- Electrical exposure.
- Pressure.
- Stored energy.
- Unexpected movement.

## Chemical Hazards

- Leaks.
- Improper storage.
- Exposure risks.

## Administrative Hazards

- Missing procedures.
- Training gaps.
- Poor communication.

---

# 8. Observe Work, Not Just Conditions

A complete inspection includes:

- Workplace conditions.
- Equipment condition.
- How work is performed.
- Whether controls are actually used.

A perfect procedure is ineffective if reality does not match it.

---

# 9. Worker Engagement

Workers often understand hazards supervisors cannot see from documentation.

Good inspection questions:

- What makes this job difficult?
- What hazards concern you?
- What would you improve?
- Have you seen anything unsafe?
- What shortcuts create pressure?

---

# 10. Positive Safety Observations

Inspections should also recognize:

- Good preparation.
- Proper PPE use.
- Correct procedures.
- Good communication.
- Safe decision-making.

A safety culture grows through reinforcement.

---

# 11. Corrective Actions

A good corrective action should identify:

- The hazard.
- The required change.
- The responsible person.
- The timeline.
- How completion will be verified.

---

# 12. Fix the System

Avoid weak corrective actions:

> "Be more careful."

Stronger actions:

- Modify equipment.
- Improve procedure.
- Remove hazard.
- Improve training.
- Add engineering controls.

---

# 13. Inspection and Incident Prevention

Inspections are an opportunity to discover hazards before:

- An injury.
- Equipment failure.
- Property damage.
- Environmental impact.

A hazard found during inspection is a success.

---

# 14. Inspection and Incident Investigation

After an incident, ask:

- Was this hazard visible?
- Should it have been identified earlier?
- Was an existing control ineffective?
- Did inspections identify similar concerns?

# 19. Escalation Triggers

Mike should escalate when:

- A serious hazard exists.
- A hazard cannot be controlled locally.
- Required resources are unavailable.
- A repeated issue continues.
- Work must stop.
- Specialized expertise is needed.

---

# 20. Stop Work Principle

A supervisor must be willing to say:

> "This condition is not acceptable. We need to correct it before continuing."

---

# 21. Inspection Decision Framework

When reviewing a hazard:

## Identify

What is unsafe?

## Assess

How severe could the outcome be?

## Control

What reduces the risk?

## Assign

Who owns correction?

## Verify

Was it actually fixed?

## Learn

Can the system improve?

---

# 22. Common Errors Claude Shall Prevent

Claude should warn against:

- Treating inspections as paperwork.
- Inspecting only after incidents.
- Focusing only on worker behaviour.
- Ignoring physical hazards.
- Closing actions without verification.
- Accepting repeated temporary fixes.
- Failing to involve workers.
- Treating a clean inspection as proof that risk does not exist.

---

# 23. Foundational Workplace Inspection Doctrine

> **Inspections are conversations with the workplace.**

> **The goal is prevention, not punishment.**

> **Finding hazards is evidence the safety system is working.**

> **Every unresolved hazard is an unanswered question.**

> **Every corrective action should improve the system.**

> **A supervisor who looks sees more. A supervisor who listens learns more.**

---

# 24. Final Statement

Workplace inspections are where safety values become visible.

Mike's questions should be:

> **What could hurt someone here?**

> **What controls are supposed to prevent that?**

> **Are those controls actually working?**

> **What are workers experiencing?**

> **What needs to improve?**

> **How do we know it is fixed?**

Safety becomes architecture when the workplace itself continuously teaches the organization how to become safer.
