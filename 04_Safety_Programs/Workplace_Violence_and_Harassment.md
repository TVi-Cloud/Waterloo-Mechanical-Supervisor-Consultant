The foundational rule:

> **Every person has the right to work in an environment that is safe, respectful, inclusive, and free from violence, harassment, and discrimination.**

Safety includes both physical and psychological safety.

# 5. Workplace Violence

Workplace violence includes behaviour or threats that could cause physical harm.

Examples may include:

- Physical assault.
- Threats of violence.
- Intimidating behaviour.
- Threatening gestures.
- Attempts to cause harm.

---

# 6. Workplace Harassment

Harassment generally involves unwanted conduct that:

- Creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment.
- Affects dignity or respect.
- Is connected to protected grounds or personal circumstances.

Always verify current University policy definitions.

---

# 7. Discrimination

Discrimination involves unequal treatment connected to protected characteristics under applicable law and University policy.

Supervisors must ensure:

- Fair treatment.
- Consistent processes.
- Respectful communication.
- Appropriate accommodation processes.

---

# 8. Supervisor Response Mindset

When an employee raises a concern:

Do not begin with:

> "Are you sure?"

Begin with:

> "Thank you for bringing this forward. Let's understand what happened and determine the appropriate next steps."

---

# 9. Listen Before Judging

A supervisor should:

- Listen.
- Gather facts.
- Avoid assumptions.
- Avoid promising outcomes.
- Explain available processes.

A supervisor is not automatically the investigator.

---

# 10. Confidentiality

Mike should protect information appropriately.

Confidentiality means:

- Share information only with those who need it.
- Avoid workplace gossip.
- Protect dignity.
- Follow University information-management requirements.

Confidentiality does not mean:

- Promising absolute secrecy.
- Ignoring mandatory escalation requirements.

---

# 11. Immediate Safety Concerns

If there is an immediate threat:

Prioritize:

1. Protect people.
2. Contact emergency resources when required.
3. Remove immediate danger where possible.
4. Notify appropriate University resources.

---

# 12. Documentation

When concerns arise, document:

- Dates.
- Times.
- Locations.
- People involved.
- Observed facts.
- Actions taken.

Avoid:

- Speculation.
- Personal judgments.
- Diagnosing motives.

---

# 13. Facts Versus Conclusions

Strong documentation:

> "Employee raised voice and stated they would damage equipment."

Weak documentation:

> "Employee was unstable and angry."

Document behaviour, not assumptions.

---

# 14. Respectful Conflict Management

Many workplace conflicts do not begin as harassment or violence.

Supervisors should encourage:

- Clear communication.
- Early discussion.
- Problem solving.
- Respectful disagreement.

Conflict handled early is easier to resolve.

---

# 15. Fairness and Consistency

Mike should apply expectations consistently.

Avoid:

- Favouritism.
- Different standards for different people.
- Ignoring behaviour from high performers.
- Overreacting to unpopular employees.

---

# 19. Retaliation Prevention

Employees should not face retaliation for:

- Raising concerns.
- Reporting hazards.
- Participating in investigations.
- Requesting support.

A safe culture depends on people being able to speak honestly.

---

# 20. Safety Culture Connection

Psychological safety affects physical safety.

Workers are more likely to report:

- Hazards.
- Near misses.
- Unsafe conditions.

when they trust their supervisor.

---

# 21. Escalation Triggers

Mike should seek guidance when:

- Violence is threatened.
- Safety is at risk.
- Harassment allegations arise.
- Discrimination concerns arise.
- Accommodation issues arise.
- Discipline may be involved.
- A conflict exceeds his authority.

---

# 22. Supervisor Decision Framework

When Mike receives a workplace conduct concern:

## Protect

Is anyone at immediate risk?

## Listen

What facts are being reported?

## Document

What objective information exists?

## Support

What does the employee need?

## Escalate

Who has the appropriate authority?

## Follow Up

Has the concern been addressed?

---

# 23. Common Errors Claude Shall Prevent

Claude should warn against:

- Ignoring complaints because they seem minor.
- Promising complete secrecy.
- Investigating beyond authority.
- Taking sides before facts are known.
- Retaliating against reporters.
- Treating respect issues as only "personality conflicts."
- Allowing workplace gossip.
- Using discipline as the first response to every conflict.
- Avoiding difficult conversations.

> **People are the most important part of the safety system.**

> **Respect is a workplace control.**

> **A worker who feels safe speaking up protects everyone.**

> **Listen first. Judge carefully. Act appropriately.**

> **Address behaviour while respecting dignity.**

> **Fairness requires consistency.**

> **Compassion and accountability are not opposites.**

> **A strong supervisor protects both the workplace and the people within it.**

# 25. Final Statement

Workplace violence and harassment prevention represents leadership in its most human form.

Mike's questions should be:

> **Is anyone unsafe?**

> **What happened?**

> **What support is needed?**

> **What authority do I have?**

> **Who needs to be involved?**

> **How do we preserve dignity while addressing the issue?**

A safe workplace is not created only through procedures and equipment.

It is created through everyday decisions about how people are treated.

That is respect becoming architecture.
