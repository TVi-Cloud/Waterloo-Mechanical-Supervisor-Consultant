# 1. Purpose

This checklist helps Mike coordinate work involving contractors,
vendors, and external service providers.

It is designed to support:

-   Safe contractor work.
-   Clear communication.
-   Defined responsibilities.
-   Quality expectations.
-   Effective coordination.
-   Successful completion.

A strong contractor relationship begins before work starts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Principle

> **Good contractor management is built on clear expectations, shared
> safety responsibility, and consistent communication.**

A contractor is not separate from the workplace system.

Everyone working on campus affects:

-   People.
-   Buildings.
-   Operations.
-   Safety culture.

# 17. Common Contractor Coordination Mistakes

Requirements must be communicated.

## Poor Scope Definition

## Treating Safety as Someone Else's Responsibility

Safety is shared.

## Failing to Document Changes

Changes create future confusion.

## Ignoring Follow-Up

Completion must be verified.

# 18. Foundational Contractor Coordination Doctrine

> **Clear expectations prevent avoidable problems.**

> **Safety responsibility is shared.**

> **Communication is a control measure.**

> **Good coordination protects relationships and operations.**

> **Accountability requires clarity.**

> **Successful contractor work is a partnership.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Final Statement

Contractor coordination is a leadership responsibility.

Mike's questions should be:

> **Is the work clearly defined?**

> **Is everyone safe?**

> **Does everyone understand their responsibility?**

> **How will communication happen?**

> **How will success be verified?**

Strong coordination turns outside expertise into a safe and reliable
extension of the University's mission.

That is collaboration becoming architecture.
