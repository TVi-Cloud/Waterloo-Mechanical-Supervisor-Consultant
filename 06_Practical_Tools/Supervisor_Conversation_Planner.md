# 1. Purpose

This tool helps Mike prepare for important workplace conversations
before they occur.

It is designed for:

-   Coaching conversations.
-   Safety discussions.
-   Performance discussions.
-   Conflict resolution.
-   Expectation setting.
-   Feedback conversations.
-   Difficult workplace discussions.

A prepared conversation is more likely to be:

-   Fair.
-   Respectful.
-   Productive.
-   Focused on improvement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Principle

> **A supervisor should prepare to understand before preparing to
> respond.**

The goal of a conversation is not simply to deliver a message.

The goal is to:

-   Understand the situation.
-   Communicate expectations.
-   Support improvement.
-   Maintain trust.
-   Achieve a constructive outcome.

# 7. Avoid Assumptions

Before the conversation, identify assumptions that need verification.

Examples:

-   "They do not care."
-   "They are intentionally ignoring expectations."
-   "They are being difficult."

Replace assumptions with questions.

# 14. Conversation Approach

Before beginning, consider:

## Tone

How should this conversation feel?

Examples:

-   Respectful.
-   Calm.
-   Direct.
-   Supportive.
-   Professional.

## Communication Style

Avoid:

-   Blame.
-   Personal attacks.
-   Assumptions.
-   Emotional reactions.

Focus on:

-   Facts.
-   Behaviour.
-   Expectations.
-   Solutions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Conversation Structure

## Opening

Explain the purpose.

"I wanted to discuss..."

## Understanding

Listen and gather perspective.

"Can you help me understand..."

## Discussion

Review facts and expectations.

## Agreement

Determine next steps.

## Closing

Confirm understanding and follow-up.

# 19. Foundational Conversation Doctrine

> **Listen first.**

> **Facts before conclusions.**

> **Respect and accountability can exist together.**

> **Difficult conversations handled well strengthen relationships.**

> **The goal is improvement, not winning.**

> **Leadership is demonstrated through how we communicate.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. Final Statement

Every conversation is an opportunity to build workplace culture.

A strong supervisor does not avoid difficult discussions.

A strong supervisor approaches them with:

-   Preparation.
-   Respect.
-   Clarity.
-   Humility.
-   Accountability.

That is communication becoming architecture.
