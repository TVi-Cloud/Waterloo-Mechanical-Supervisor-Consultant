# 1. Purpose

This document is a supervisor-focused intelligence layer for University of Waterloo Policy 36 — Dispute Resolution for University Support Staff.

It helps Claude and Mike:

- Identify whether Policy 36 applies.
- Distinguish a Policy 36 dispute from a CUPE Local 793 grievance, a Policy 33 complaint, a safety matter, or another process.
- Respond properly when a staff member questions management action or inaction.
- Preserve confidentiality, procedural fairness, records, and timelines.
- Understand the informal discussion, mediation, and formal Tribunal stages.
- Recognize when Human Resources, the Secretariat, the Conflict Management Office, or another authority must be involved.

This document does not replace the official policy, legal advice, Human Resources advice, or the CUPE Local 793 Collective Agreement.

---

# 2. Critical Applicability Rule

Policy 36 applies to a **regular staff member, as defined in Policy 54, who has completed probation**.

A probationary staff member is directed to contact the University of Waterloo Staff Association and/or Human Resources for advice.

Mike primarily supervises employees represented by **CUPE Local 793**. A CUPE member's employment dispute will ordinarily be governed by the grievance and arbitration provisions of the CUPE Local 793 Collective Agreement, not Policy 36.

> **Claude shall never route a CUPE Local 793 employee into Policy 36 merely because the issue concerns a management decision or University policy.**

Before recommending any process, Claude must determine:

1. The employee's bargaining-unit or employment status.
2. Whether probation has been completed.
3. The nature of the concern.
4. The controlling policy, collective agreement, statute, or procedure.
5. Whether another process has already begun.

If status or jurisdiction is uncertain, Claude shall recommend prompt consultation with Human Resources, Labour Relations, the employee's union or association representative, and/or the Secretariat as appropriate.

# 4. Policy Objective

Policy 36 provides a confidential, effective, and fair way to resolve problems, disputes, misunderstandings, and disagreements about management action or inaction that directly affects an eligible staff member.

Examples may include an alleged failure to apply or misapplication of a University policy, procedure, or guideline involving:

- Abuse of supervisory authority.
- Demotion.
- Working conditions.
- Discipline.
- Discharge.

The examples are not a substitute for a jurisdiction assessment.

---

# 5. Governing Principles

Policy 36 recognizes that:

- Staff may question management decisions in confidence and without reprisal or fear of reprisal.
- Questions, concerns, and debate are healthy and encouraged.
- Concerns should be addressed in good faith, informally, and promptly where possible.
- Resources are available to assist the parties.
- A staff member may proceed formally if informal resolution fails.
- A staff member may bypass the informal stage and proceed directly to the formal stage.
- Everyone involved must maintain confidentiality and behave professionally.

Mike shall treat a dispute as a process requiring calm, fairness, and disciplined documentation—not as disloyalty or insubordination merely because a management decision is challenged.

---

# 6. No-Reprisal Rule

A staff member has the right to question a management decision without reprisal or fear of reprisal.

Mike shall not retaliate, threaten, disadvantage, isolate, schedule punitively, deny opportunities, intensify scrutiny, or otherwise treat a person adversely because they raised or supported a dispute in good faith.

Normal supervision may continue, but decisions must remain operationally justified, consistent, and documented.

Any concern about reprisal shall be escalated promptly to Human Resources and assessed for overlap with Policy 33 or other applicable authority.

---

# 7. Who Initiates the Process

Responsibility for initiating Policy 36 rests with the staff member experiencing the problem.

A group of staff members may bring a group complaint when they seek resolution of the same issue.

Mike does not initiate a Policy 36 dispute on an employee's behalf. He must, however:

- Receive concerns professionally.
- Avoid obstructing access to the process.
- Respond within applicable timelines.
- Preserve relevant information.
- Participate in good faith.

---

# 8. Support Persons and Lawyers

At any stage, both the staff member and the manager may receive assistance from and be accompanied by a support person chosen from regular University staff.

Depending on the party's needs, a support person may:

- Provide emotional support.
- Help navigate the process.
- Speak on behalf of the party.

Parties may consult a lawyer for advice, but Policy 36 states that lawyers may not attend any stage of the process.

Claude shall verify this rule against the current live policy before a hearing or meeting.

---

# 9. Correct Process Routing

Before responding on substance, Claude shall classify the matter.

| Situation | Likely Primary Route |
|---|---|
| Eligible regular support staff member challenges management action or inaction | Policy 36 |
| CUPE Local 793 employee challenges discipline, interpretation, or working conditions | CUPE grievance procedure |
| Alleged harassment, discrimination, sexual violence, workplace violence, reprisal, or abuse of authority | Policy 33 and its procedures, with the employment-group resolution route where applicable |
| Immediate health or safety hazard or work refusal | Policy 34, OHSA requirements, and safety procedures |
| Human-rights accommodation issue | Human Rights Code, accommodation process, and applicable employment framework |
| Privacy, records, or information-management concern | Applicable privacy law and Policy 46 or related procedures |
| Probationary regular staff concern | Staff Association and/or Human Resources advice; Policy 36 eligibility must be checked |
| External legal or administrative proceeding has begun | Obtain institutional advice immediately; Policy 36 may cease |

Some matters engage more than one authority. Claude shall identify the overlap rather than forcing the issue into a single category.

---

# 10. Informal Stage — Discussion Phase

The discussion phase begins when the staff member raises a concern with the manager whose action or inaction is being questioned.

The communication may be oral or written and must refer to the informal stage of Policy 36.

The manager must:

1. Meet with the staff member.
2. Discuss the concern in good faith.
3. Respond with reasons.
4. Do so within **five working days** of receiving the complaint.

For this policy, working days are Monday through Friday, excluding statutory and University holidays.

The parties may agree in writing to suspend the five-working-day response limit for a reasonable period to facilitate informal resolution.

---

# 11. Manager Response Protocol

When Mike receives a Policy 36 concern, he should:

1. Record the date and method of receipt.
2. Confirm the employee's status and Policy 36 eligibility.
3. Identify the challenged action or inaction.
4. Clarify the policy, procedure, or guideline said to have been misapplied.
5. Preserve relevant records, emails, notes, schedules, work orders, and decision materials.
6. Notify and consult Human Resources or Line Management where appropriate.
7. Arrange a private meeting within the required period.
8. Listen without interruption or defensiveness.
9. Check factual assumptions and consider corrective options.
10. Provide clear reasons for the response.
11. Document the meeting and outcome factually.

Claude shall not fabricate a justification after the fact or recommend altering records to strengthen management's position.

---

# 12. Informal Outcomes

Possible informal outcomes include:

- Explaining the action to the staff member's satisfaction.
- Modifying the action.
- Reversing the action.
- Using another resolution method.
- Escalating the concern to the next management level.
- Proceeding to mediation.
- Proceeding to the formal stage.

Changing or reversing a decision after fair review is not an admission of personal failure. It may be the correct institutional response to incomplete information, an error, or a better-supported alternative.

---

# 13. Informal Stage — Mediation

Mediation may help the parties resolve the issue with a third party.

Mediation requires agreement by both the staff member and the manager. Trained mediators may be accessed through the Conflict Management Office and Human Resources.

Mike shall not:

- Coerce an employee to mediate.
- Treat refusal to mediate as misconduct.
- Use mediation to delay a matter until the formal filing deadline expires.
- Assume that mediation suspends a formal deadline unless a valid written arrangement or applicable rule confirms this.

---

# 14. Formal Stage — Filing Deadlines

An eligible staff member may request a Tribunal hearing without completing the informal stage.

The current published deadlines are:

- **Termination:** within 10 working days after receiving notice of termination.
- **All other situations:** within 60 working days from the event.

Informal resolution has no separate completion deadline, but staff must remain aware of the formal-stage deadlines.

A Tribunal may extend a time limit when non-compliance has not substantially prejudiced either the staff member's or the University's position. Mike and Claude shall never assume an extension will be granted.

---

# 15. Form 36A

The formal process is requested through the current **Request for a Dispute Resolution Hearing — Form 36A**, submitted to the Secretariat.

The request includes:

- A brief explanation of the issue.
- The policy, procedure, or guideline under which the dispute is filed.
- The remedy sought.
- A chronology of resolution attempts, if any.

The current live form and submission instructions must be retrieved from the Secretariat at the time of use. Claude shall not rely on an old saved form, address, or contact.

---

# 16. Formal-Stage Sequence

The current published sequence includes the following core deadlines:

| Step | Current Published Timing |
|---|---|
| Staff member files Form 36A | 10 working days after termination notice, or 60 working days from another event |
| Secretariat begins Tribunal-establishment process | Within 2 working days after receipt |
| Staff member accepts or challenges proposed Tribunal composition | Within 2 working days after notification |
| Tribunal determines whether the matter can be heard | Within 5 working days after formation |
| Staff member submits additional information if matter will be heard | Within 5 working days after decision to hear |
| Manager submits response, documents, witnesses, and support-person information | Within 5 working days after receiving the staff member's material |
| Tribunal holds organizational in-camera meeting | Within 10 working days after formation of the agreed hearing Tribunal |
| Hearing occurs | Within 20 working days after the decision to hear |
| Secretariat normally distributes the Tribunal report | Within 5 working days after the decision |

This table is an operational aid only. The official policy, Appendix A, Tribunal directions, and Secretariat communications control.

# 17. Tribunal Jurisdiction

The Tribunal first decides whether the issue can be heard under Policy 36.

If it hears the case, its mandate is to determine relevant facts and apply a reasonable interpretation of the applicable University policy, procedure, or guideline.

Possible formal outcomes are:

- Confirming the management action.
- Modifying the action.
- Reversing the action.

The Tribunal's decision is final and binding on the parties under the policy.

---

# 18. Natural Justice and Procedural Fairness

The Tribunal controls its process, informed by natural justice.

The parties have the right to:

- Notice of the hearing.
- Be heard.
- Name witnesses.
- Question witnesses.
- Receive written reasons.
- Have the matter decided by an objective and unbiased decision-maker.

Mike's conduct before the hearing should support these rights. He shall not conceal relevant evidence, coach witnesses to misstate facts, contact Tribunal members privately, or interfere with participation.

---

# 19. Tribunal Composition and Challenges

A Tribunal consists of a Chair and two members from the Staff Dispute Resolution Pool selected by the Secretariat using a rotating list and considering prior involvement and conflicts of interest.

Both parties have opportunities under Appendix A to accept or challenge proposed members.

A challenge should be based on a genuine concern about impartiality, conflict, or prior involvement—not tactical delay or dissatisfaction with a person's general background.

---

# 20. Evidence and Disclosure

The manager's written response should be complete, relevant, factual, and supported by contemporaneous records.

Potential evidence may include:

- The decision and reasons communicated at the time.
- Relevant University policies and procedures.
- Emails and correspondence.
- Meeting notes.
- Schedules, assignments, work orders, and operational records.
- Prior expectations or instructions.
- Comparable treatment, where relevant and appropriate.
- Witness names and the evidence each witness can provide.

Parties generally receive the material presented to the Tribunal. The Tribunal may restrict access where disclosure would be inappropriate or violate confidentiality, such as information identifying co-workers or personal medical information, and must provide reasons.

Mike shall send formal-stage materials through the Secretariat as directed rather than distributing them broadly.

---

# 21. Records Preservation

As soon as a dispute is raised or reasonably anticipated, Mike shall preserve potentially relevant records.

He shall not:

- Delete or overwrite messages or notes.
- Backdate records.
- Rewrite contemporaneous notes without retaining the original.
- Ask others to destroy or alter information.
- Gather unrelated personal information.
- Circulate dispute materials beyond those with a legitimate need to know.

Late-created explanatory notes must be clearly identified as later recollections, not contemporaneous records.

---

# 22. Confidentiality

All participants must hold dispute-resolution matters in confidence and conduct themselves professionally.

Confidentiality means limiting information to people who need it for advice, representation, decision-making, evidence, or process administration. It does not mean promising absolute secrecy where disclosure is legally or institutionally required.

Mike should:

- Meet privately.
- Store records securely.
- Avoid discussing the dispute with uninvolved staff.
- Share only what is necessary.
- Seek advice before disclosing medical, human-rights, safety, or third-party information.

---

# 23. Remedies and Limits

A manager or Tribunal cannot amend or add to Policy 36 or another University policy, procedure, or guideline, and cannot issue a decision inconsistent with those authorities.

Financial remedies beyond those provided by University policy are unavailable under Policy 36. Monetary compensation may be awarded only when it flows from the application of another University policy. Compensation for mental distress or pain and suffering is not available through this process.

Claude shall distinguish between:

- The remedy a person requests.
- The remedy Policy 36 legally and institutionally permits.
- Any remedy available through a different process.

---

# 24. External Proceedings

Policy 36 states that anyone may begin legal action. However, if a party starts legal action or takes the issue to an external agency, the Policy 36 process ceases.

Because external routes and institutional offices can change, Mike shall immediately seek advice from Human Resources and the appropriate University office if an external claim, application, investigation, demand, or legal proceeding is mentioned or received.

Claude shall not tell a person that Policy 36 removes or limits their statutory or legal rights.

---

# 25. Hearing Conduct

At a hearing, the staff member presents first, including the claim, requested remedy, testimony, witnesses, and documents. The manager then presents the response, justification, testimony, witnesses, and documents. Reply evidence and questioning occur as directed by the Tribunal.

Mike should:

- Answer questions directly and truthfully.
- Distinguish personal knowledge from assumption or hearsay.
- Explain the reasoning that existed when the decision was made.
- Acknowledge errors or missing information.
- Avoid exaggeration and character attacks.
- Remain professional when evidence is disputed.
- Follow the Chair's directions.

---

# 26. Extensions and Procedural Directions

If a party cannot meet a formal-stage deadline, the request for an extension must be made in writing to the Chair before the deadline and state the reasons and requested length.

The Tribunal considers the adequacy of the reasons and possible prejudice. The Tribunal may alter procedural time limits, and procedural requirements may be waived with the consent required by the policy.

Mike shall not treat an extension as automatic. He should raise scheduling or disclosure problems immediately.

---

# 27. Interaction With Policy 33

Policy 33 identifies different resolution routes by community group. For staff, Policy 36 may be the employment-related resolution route; for CUPE Local 793 members, the collective agreement is the identified route.

Where facts may involve harassment, discrimination, sexual violence, reprisal, abuse of authority, or another Policy 33 concern:

1. Do not reduce the matter to an ordinary disagreement.
2. Consult the current Policy 33 and applicable procedures.
3. Determine whether protective or interim measures are needed.
4. Preserve confidentiality and prohibit reprisal.
5. Identify the correct employment-group route.
6. Seek advice from the appropriate University office.

Policy 36 and Policy 33 may intersect, but they are not interchangeable.

---

# 28. Interaction With the CUPE Local 793 Collective Agreement

For a CUPE Local 793 employee, the collective agreement may govern disputes involving:

- Discipline or discharge.
- Hours, overtime, call-ins, or scheduling.
- Classification or posting rights.
- Seniority.
- Leave.
- Management-rights interpretation.
- Application or alleged violation of the collective agreement.

Mike shall:

- Recognize the employee's right to union representation.
- Follow collective-agreement timelines.
- Consult Labour Relations or Human Resources promptly.
- Avoid substituting Policy 36 for the contractual grievance process.
- Avoid bypassing the union where the agreement requires union involvement.

Policy 36 may provide useful fairness principles, but it is not the controlling grievance procedure for CUPE employees unless current institutional authorities expressly establish otherwise.

# 29. Supervisor Decision Rules

Claude shall use the following rules when advising Mike:

### Rule 1 — Identify the employee group first

Never select a dispute process before confirming whether the person is CUPE Local 793, regular support staff, probationary staff, faculty, student, contractor, or another category.

### Rule 2 — Start the clock immediately

Record receipt dates and calculate working-day deadlines using the live policy and University calendar.

### Rule 3 — Do not block access

An employee may bypass informal resolution. Do not imply that a private meeting, mediation, or management appeal is mandatory before filing formally.

### Rule 4 — Respond with reasons

A bare conclusion is not a sufficient managerial response. Explain the facts, authority, and operational reasoning.

### Rule 5 — Correct errors early

If a decision was based on incorrect facts or misapplied authority, consider modifying or reversing it promptly with appropriate consultation.

### Rule 6 — Preserve evidence and confidentiality

Keep records intact, factual, secure, and limited to those with a legitimate role.

### Rule 7 — No reprisal

Separate the dispute from ongoing supervision and scrutinize later decisions for retaliation risk.

### Rule 8 — Escalate jurisdiction questions

Where Policy 36, Policy 33, Policy 34, accommodation law, the collective agreement, or an external proceeding may overlap, seek institutional advice.

- Assume all employees supervised by Mike are eligible under Policy 36.

# 32. Immediate Escalation Triggers

Mike should seek prompt institutional advice when:

- Employment status or process jurisdiction is unclear.
- Termination, suspension, or serious discipline is involved.
- A formal deadline may be close or disputed.
- The employee alleges reprisal, discrimination, harassment, violence, abuse of authority, or accommodation failure.
- A group complaint is raised.
- Medical or highly sensitive personal information is involved.
- An external agency, lawyer, court, regulator, police service, or legal demand is involved.
- Records may be missing, altered, or subject to a preservation obligation.
- A conflict of interest or reasonable apprehension of bias exists.
- A settlement or remedy may exceed Mike's authority.
- The matter affects safety, statutory compliance, or critical operations.

# 33. Practical Intake Checklist

When a concern is received, capture only the information needed to route and respond:

- Date and time received.
- Employee name and employment group.
- Probation status, if relevant.
- Whether Policy 36 was expressly invoked.
- Challenged action or inaction.
- Date of the event or termination notice.
- Policy, procedure, guideline, or agreement provision identified.
- Remedy requested.
- Informal steps already attempted.
- Representation or support person requested.
- Immediate safety, accommodation, human-rights, or reprisal concerns.
- Related external or internal proceedings.
- Records requiring preservation.
- Advice requested from Human Resources, Labour Relations, the Secretariat, or another office.

The checklist is not a substitute for the official Form 36A or any formal intake process.

# 34. Suggested Manager Communication Pattern

A sound initial response usually:

1. Acknowledges receipt without deciding the merits.
2. Confirms the date and the process the employee invoked.
3. States that reprisal is prohibited and confidentiality will be respected within process requirements.
4. Confirms any requested support person or representation.
5. Sets a private meeting within the required time.
6. Identifies any immediate records or information needed.
7. Avoids promising an outcome.
8. Provides a written record of next steps.

Claude may draft communications using this pattern, but must not insert facts Mike has not supplied.

# 35. Source Hierarchy

For actual decisions, Claude shall apply the following hierarchy contextually:

1. Applicable legislation and binding legal requirements.
2. Applicable collective agreement or other binding employment agreement.
3. Current University policies.
4. Current University procedures and guidelines.
5. Tribunal or authorized institutional directions in the specific matter.
6. Plant Operations procedures and documented local practices.
7. Supervisory discretion.
8. This repository interpretation.

Where authorities conflict or jurisdiction is unclear, Claude shall identify the conflict and recommend escalation rather than choosing casually.

# 36. Final Operating Doctrine

Policy 36 is not merely a complaint mechanism. It is a structured fairness system.

For Mike, the correct supervisory posture is:

> **Identify the right process, listen without reprisal, respond with reasons, preserve the record, respect the clock, and let fair procedure test the decision.**

The first and most important question is always:

> **Is this employee and this issue actually governed by Policy 36?**

For Mike's CUPE Local 793 workforce, the answer will often be no—the collective-agreement grievance procedure will usually be the controlling route.
