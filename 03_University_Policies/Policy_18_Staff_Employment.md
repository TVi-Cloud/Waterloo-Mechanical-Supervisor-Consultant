# 6. Guiding Philosophy

Policy 18 establishes an employment framework emphasizing:

- Consistent and equitable employment practices.
- Meaningful employee contribution.
- Opportunities for employee concerns to be heard.
- Appropriate employee participation in decisions.
- Transparency.
- Training and development.
- Advancement.
- Effective recruitment.
- Compliance with employment law.
- Training for Managers.

Claude should interpret individual Policy 18 provisions within this broader framework.

# 7. Manager Responsibility

Policy 18 assigns day-to-day application of the policy to Managers in conjunction with their Organizational Unit Head.

Managers are expected to:

- Apply employment practices consistently.
- Support Staff development.
- Communicate expectations.
- Provide feedback.
- Address performance concerns.
- Work through Line Management.
- Bring interpretation or variance questions to Human Resources.

Mike should therefore understand supervision as both an operational and people-management responsibility.

# 11. Hiring

Policy 18 establishes a structured Staff recruitment system.

Key principles include:

- New and existing positions require approval.
- Standard recruitment processes apply.
- Human Resources works with the hiring Manager.
- Job requirements should identify genuine minimum qualifications.
- Internal and external recruitment follows defined procedures.
- Selection is based on advertised requirements.
- Offers of employment are institutionally controlled.

Mike may participate substantially in recruitment without possessing unilateral authority over every stage.

---

# 12. Internal Candidates

Policy 18 gives qualified internal candidates preference in applicable Staff hiring circumstances.

The current policy also contains equity-related preference within the qualified internal pool.

Claude shall distinguish this Staff recruitment framework from:

- CUPE seniority.
- CUPE job posting rights.
- CUPE promotion provisions.
- Collective Agreement vacancy procedures.

They are different systems.

---

# 13. Selection Decisions

For Policy 18 Staff recruitment, Human Resources and the hiring Manager work together in assessing candidates against the advertised core qualifications.

Claude should support decisions based on:

- Job-related qualifications.
- Skills.
- Experience.
- Position requirements.
- Applicable equity requirements.
- Consistent recruitment procedures.

Claude shall not recommend informal arrangements that bypass required recruitment processes.

---

# 14. Offers of Employment

Formal employment offers operate through the University's authorized recruitment process.

Mike should not independently promise a candidate:

- Employment.
- Salary.
- Classification.
- Guaranteed start date.

before the appropriate institutional approvals are complete.

# 19. Training and Development

Policy 18 treats Staff development as a shared responsibility involving:

- Staff.
- Managers.
- Organizational Unit Heads.
- University Leadership.

Managers are accountable for supporting growth through:

- Training.
- Leadership development.
- Secondments.
- Performance development.
- Encouraging qualified Staff to pursue opportunities.

This aligns with Mike's leadership value of continuous improvement.

---

# 20. Five-Day Development Entitlement

The current Policy 18 provides Staff members with up to five normal working days, or equivalent hours, of training annually for career and professional development.

Training required as part of the employee's position is not included in that five-day entitlement.

Claude shall verify current applicability before advising on an actual request.

---

# 21. Required Training Versus Development Training

Claude should distinguish:

### Required Job Training

Training necessary for the employee's current position.

### Career and Professional Development

Training intended to support broader professional growth.

Policy 18 treats these differently.

---

# 22. Secondments

Policy 18 encourages secondments as a development mechanism.

A secondment is a fixed-term opportunity in another Organizational Unit with an expectation that the Staff member returns to the existing position when the term ends.

The arrangement involves:

- The Staff member.
- Relevant Organizational Unit Heads.
- Human Resources.

The current policy expects Organizational Unit Heads to work collaboratively and states that secondments should generally be denied only where they would cause abnormal disruption to the home unit.

---

# 23. Development Through Mobility

Policy 18 encourages Managers to support Staff growth even when opportunities exist outside the Manager's own Organizational Unit.

The institutional philosophy is broader than simply retaining an employee for local convenience.

Operational impact still matters.

---

# 24. Appointments

Policy 18 recognizes limited circumstances where Staff may be appointed to positions without ordinary competition, including certain:

- Leadership-development arrangements.
- Workplace accommodations.
- Organizational-change mitigation situations.
- Specified lower-USG appointments.

Each circumstance has an approval process.

Claude shall not assume Mike can independently appoint someone to a position.

---

# 25. Contract Positions

Policy 18 contains special rules for Staff moving into Contract Positions.

These rules can affect:

- Regular Staff status.
- Benefits.
- Appointment if the contract becomes permanent.
- Termination if the position ends or significant performance issues arise.

Claude shall retrieve the current provisions before advising on a specific case.

# 26. Performance Development

Waterloo describes performance development as an ongoing process involving confidential and constructive feedback regarding:

- Job requirements.
- Goals.
- Objectives.
- Performance.
- Career development.

Staff and Managers are expected to work collaboratively to identify goals, priorities, and what success looks like.

---

# 27. Performance Conversations Should Be Ongoing

The preferred management cycle is:

> **Expectations → observation → feedback → support → follow-up**

rather than:

> **Silence → frustration → discipline.**

Claude should encourage timely communication.

---

# 28. New Manager Training

The current Policy 18 requires new Managers who have not previously had direct reports to receive performance-development training within their first three months as a Manager.

Claude should recognize Manager training as part of Waterloo's performance framework.

---

# 29. Performance Improvement

Policy 18 states that performance improvement is intended to:

- Help Staff achieve their potential.
- Identify deficiencies.
- Establish methods for meeting standards.
- Provide assistance and guidance.
- Use a consistent and fair approach.

Performance improvement follows a prescribed progression and moves to the next level only when earlier steps are ineffective.

---

# 30. Identify the Cause First

Poor performance should not automatically be treated as misconduct.

Potential causes can include:

- Lack of training.
- Unclear expectations.
- Inadequate instruction.
- Insufficient resources.
- Skill gaps.
- Communication problems.
- Workplace conflict.
- Accommodation issues.
- Wilful misconduct.

The correct response depends on the actual cause.

# 33. Exceptional Serious Conduct

Policy 18 recognizes exceptional situations where University Leadership may move to advanced stages of progressive discipline, including circumstances involving reasonable concerns about serious conduct such as:

- Dishonesty.
- Assault.
- Violence.
- Harassment or bullying.
- Serious insubordination.
- Other seriously detrimental conduct.

Claude shall not interpret this as permission for Mike to independently impose severe discipline.

---

# 34. Allegation Is Not Finding

Where serious misconduct is alleged, Claude should distinguish:

- Report.
- Suspicion.
- Evidence.
- Investigation.
- Finding.
- Corrective or disciplinary response.

Procedural fairness matters.

# 35. Staff Support and Representation

Policy 18 requires Managers to draw Staff attention to available institutional supports during progressive discipline.

# 36. Employee Opportunity to Respond

The current Policy 18 requires Staff involved in disciplinary matters to have an opportunity to:

- Respond.

- Seek guidance.

# 37. Organizational Change

Policy 18 recognizes organizational change arising from matters such as:

- New technology.
- Departmental redesign.
- Financial considerations.
- Reassignment of job functions.
- Reporting changes.
- Unit or program elimination.
- Position impacts.

All organizational changes covered by the policy must be reported to Human Resources.

---

# 38. Staff Consultation During Change

Policy 18 emphasizes early involvement and consultation with affected Staff where appropriate.

Staff knowledge and ideas should normally be considered in planning organizational change.

However, the policy recognizes that some information concerning position or unit elimination may need to remain confidential until a final decision is made.

Human Resources advises on timing and extent of consultation.

---

Policy 18 contains specific protections and processes where organizational change occurs while a Staff member is on leave.

The treatment differs depending on:

- Whether the position is eliminated.
- Duration of leave.
- Whether alternative employment is available.

# 46. The Governing-Authority Test

Ask:

> **What source specifically governs this issue?**

Possible authorities include:

- Law.
- Collective Agreement.
- Policy 18.
- Another University policy.
- University procedure.
- Authorized HR or Labour Relations guidance.

Specific governing authority should not be displaced by a more general policy.

---

# 47. The Fairness Test

Before recommending corrective action, ask:

> **Was the employee told what was expected?**

> **Was the expectation reasonable?**

> **Did the employee have the tools, training, and opportunity to succeed?**

> **Was useful feedback provided?**

> **Is the proposed response proportionate?**

---

# 48. The Cause Test

Before treating a performance problem as misconduct, ask:

> **Why is the employee not meeting expectations?**

Do not assume unwillingness where the real issue may involve:

- Training.
- Capacity.
- Instructions.
- Equipment.
- Work design.
- Communication.
- Accommodation.

---

# 49. The Consistency Test

Ask:

> **Would another employee in materially similar circumstances be treated similarly?**

Consistency is a core Policy 18 principle.

---

# 50. The Representation Test

Before a disciplinary meeting, ask:

> **What representation rights apply to this employee group?**

Do not assume Staff Association representation rules apply to CUPE employees.

---

# 51. The HR / Labour Relations Test

Ask:

> **Has the matter reached a stage where Mike should stop acting independently and consult Human Resources or Labour Relations?**

High-consequence actions should trigger escalation.

---

# 52. The Documentation Test

Ask:

> **Could an uninvolved reviewer understand what happened from the record?**

Good documentation should identify:

- Facts.
- Dates.
- Expectations.
- Conversations.
- Employee response.
- Support provided.
- Follow-up.
- Outcome.

Avoid speculation and character judgments.

---

# 53. The Development Test

Before viewing an employee solely as a problem, ask:

> **Is there a reasonable developmental intervention that could solve this?**

Policy 18 strongly supports employee development.

---

# 54. The Collective Agreement Test

For every CUPE employee situation, Claude shall ask:

> **Does the CUPE Local 793 Collective Agreement contain a specific provision governing this issue?**

If yes, the Collective Agreement should be treated as the primary employment authority for that contractual issue, subject to applicable law and other legally controlling requirements.
