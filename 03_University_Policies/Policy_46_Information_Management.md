- Hard-copy documents.

# 8. Information Lifecycle

Policy 46 manages information throughout its lifecycle:

1. Creation or collection.
2. Capture.
3. Organization and classification.
4. Storage.
5. Retrieval.
6. Use.
7. Sharing or dissemination.
8. Retention.
9. Final disposition.
10. Long-term preservation where required.

Good information management therefore begins when information is created, not when somebody eventually decides to delete it.

# 9. The Four Information Roles

Policy 46 establishes four key information-management roles:

- Information Steward.
- Information Custodian.
- Information User.
- Information Service Provider.

Claude should identify Mike's actual role in relation to a particular information asset before recommending action.

---

# 10. Information Steward

An Information Steward is the owner of information in University custody, or a senior-level employee delegated responsibility for a collection of information.

Stewards are responsible for matters such as:

- Creation.
- Maintenance.
- Protection.
- Use.
- Standards.
- Procedures.
- Lifecycle controls.
- Approval of disposal or destruction.

Mike should not assume he is the Information Steward merely because information concerns his employees or operations.

---

# 11. Information Custodian

Information Custodians have delegated operational responsibility for a collection of information.

Their responsibilities include matters such as:

- Implementing controls.
- Managing authorized access.
- Granting and revoking access as approved.
- Supporting lifecycle management.
- Supporting risk management.
- Detecting and reporting security incidents.

Mike may have custodian-like operational responsibilities for some information, but Claude shall verify rather than assume formal designation.

---

# 12. Information User

An Information User accesses and uses information in University custody or control.

Mike will frequently act as an Information User.

Users are responsible for:

- Following approved rules and procedures.
- Respecting implemented controls.
- Using information only as authorized.
- Reporting security breaches they become aware of as quickly as possible.

Access does not equal unrestricted authority.

---

# 13. Information Service Provider

Information Service Providers design, manage, or support systems and services used to manage University information.

Examples can include authorized IT personnel or contractors.

Their technical access does not mean they own the information or can use it for unrelated purposes.

# 15. Accountability and Accessibility

The University has public-accountability obligations.

Non-confidential information about University programs, services, and governance may appropriately be available.

At the same time, public accountability does not mean every University record is public.

Claude must distinguish:

> **University information**

from:

> **information authorized for public disclosure.**

# 16. Access Is Need- and Permission-Based

Employees should have timely access to information they need to perform their responsibilities, provided appropriate permissions exist and legal requirements are respected.

Therefore:

> **Need to know is not the same as curiosity.**

Mike should access employee or institutional information because it is necessary for legitimate work, not merely because he technically can.

The University protects confidential information consistent with applicable:

- Law.
- Regulation.
- Contracts.
- Agreements.
- University requirements.

This can include:

- Personal information.
- Employment information.
- Commercially sensitive information.

# 18. Information Classification

Policy 46 distinguishes:

- Public.
- Confidential.
- Restricted.
- Highly Restricted.

Claude should identify or verify classification before recommending storage, sharing, transmission, or use.

---

# 19. Public Information

Public information is information to which no other security classification has been applied.

Even public information should still be used accurately and in accordance with University responsibilities.

Public classification does not authorize someone to impersonate an official University spokesperson.

---

# 20. Confidential Information

Confidential information is information expected not to be disclosed except to people requiring it for a legitimate purpose.

Examples in Mike's context may include some:

- Employee-management information.
- Complaint information.
- Performance information.
- Investigation information.
- Operational information.

Exact classification should be verified against current University standards.

---

# 21. Restricted Information

Restricted information is a subset of Confidential information requiring protection because of legal or regulatory obligations or because inappropriate use may trigger notification requirements.

Restricted information generally requires stronger controls than ordinary Confidential information.

---

# 22. Highly Restricted Information

Highly Restricted information is the highest-risk subset of Restricted information and requires heightened protection and restrictions.

Policy 46 gives examples such as information commonly used for identity theft, including:

- Social Insurance Numbers.
- Bank account numbers.

Mike should rarely need to possess this type of information in ordinary Mechanical Services supervision.

If he encounters it unexpectedly:

> **Do not copy, redistribute, or upload it unnecessarily.**

---

# 23. Data Minimization

Although Policy 46 is broader than a single privacy rule, a useful supervisory principle is:

> **Use only the information reasonably necessary for the legitimate task.**

If Mike can resolve an issue without collecting or sharing additional personal information, avoid unnecessary collection.

---

# 24. Information Quality

Policy 46 emphasizes information quality.

Administrative records should, as much as possible:

- Be accurate.
- Be created within a reasonable time of the event.
- Come from authoritative sources.
- Clearly distinguish official records from transitory material.

Claude should encourage contemporaneous, factual documentation.

---

# 25. Authoritative Sources

Official and authoritative sources should be known and accessible.

For this project, that principle is critical.

They are not automatically the authoritative University record.

Claude must live-check authoritative sources for consequential decisions.

# 27. No Shadow Personnel File

Mike should not create an uncontrolled private personnel archive outside approved University systems.

Examples of risky practices include:

- Keeping sensitive employee files indefinitely on a personal laptop.
- Maintaining an unofficial dossier for future discipline.
- Saving employee information in unauthorized cloud storage.
- Forwarding records to a personal email account.

Where the official employment record belongs with Human Resources or another authorized custodian, follow the approved process.

---

# 28. Facts, Not Character Judgments

A good record says:

> **Employee arrived at 8:17 a.m. for a scheduled 8:00 a.m. start.**

A poor record says:

> **Employee is lazy and doesn't care.**

Claude should help Mike document observable facts, relevant context, expectations, employee response, and action taken.

# 37. Access Control

- Transfers.
- Leaves the unit.

# 38. Shared Accounts and Credentials

Mike should not normalize:

- Shared passwords.
- Credential sharing.
- Posting passwords visibly.
- Sending credentials through insecure channels.

# 39. Information Security Breach

Policy 46 defines an Information Security Breach as involving one or more of:

- Circumvention of information-security controls.
- Unauthorized use of information.
- Unintended exposure of information.

Examples may include:

- Misdirected email.
- Lost device.
- Unauthorized file access.
- Accidental public sharing.
- Stolen records.
- Improper disclosure.
- Compromised credentials.

---

# 40. Breach Response Rule

If Mike suspects an information-security or privacy breach:

> **Do not quietly fix it and say nothing.**

Instead:

1. Limit further exposure where safe and authorized.
2. Preserve relevant facts.
3. Report promptly through the current University breach process.
4. Follow instructions from responsible University authorities.
5. Do not independently notify affected individuals unless instructed through the authorized process.

---

# 41. Breach Documentation

Useful initial facts include:

- What information was involved.
- Who may have been affected.
- How exposure occurred.
- When it occurred.
- Who may have accessed it.
- Whether access can be revoked.
- What immediate containment occurred.
- Who was notified.

Do not delay reporting while attempting to produce a perfect investigation.

---

# 42. Security Incident Versus Discipline

An information-security incident may involve:

- Human error.
- Training gaps.
- Process failure.
- Technical failure.
- Deliberate misconduct.

Claude should not assume every breach is disciplinary misconduct.

Containment and institutional reporting come first.

Employment consequences are analyzed separately.

# 43. Records Classification

# 44. Retention

Do not retain records based solely on:

> **Maybe I'll need this someday.**

Retention should follow:

- Legal requirements.
- Operational requirements.
- University retention schedules.
- Applicable holds or investigations.

Keeping information forever can create risk as well as convenience.

---

# 45. Transitory Records

Policy 46 recognizes that some information does not need to be retained once its temporary purpose ends.

Transitory information should be securely destroyed on a routine basis according to current University guidance.

Claude should distinguish:

> **Official record**

from:

> **temporary working material.**

---

# 46. Do Not Destroy Records Because They Are Inconvenient

A record should never be deleted or destroyed merely because:

- It reflects poorly on someone.
- A complaint was made.
- Litigation is feared.
- A grievance is expected.
- An access request may occur.
- An investigation is underway.

Retention, legal hold, grievance, investigation, or other requirements may prohibit disposal.

When uncertain:

> **Do not destroy; escalate.**

---

# 47. Secure Destruction

Secure destruction means information is protected against unauthorized use before destruction and destroyed irreversibly so it cannot reasonably be recovered or reconstructed.

Examples may require approved:

- Shredding.
- Secure media destruction.
- Authorized electronic deletion processes.

Moving a file to a computer trash folder is not necessarily secure destruction.

---

# 56. AI and University Information

AI introduces an additional information-use decision.

Before using Claude with real University information, ask:

1. Is the information Public, Confidential, Restricted, or Highly Restricted?
2. Is this AI environment approved for that classification?
3. Is disclosure to the AI provider authorized?
4. Is the information necessary to solve the problem?
5. Can identifying details be removed?
6. Is a hypothetical version sufficient?
7. Is the output going to create a new University record?
8. Where should any useful output ultimately be stored?

If these questions cannot be answered:

> **Do not expose the information to the AI system.**

# 62. The Need-to-Know Test

Before accessing or sharing information, ask:

> **Does this person need this information to perform a legitimate University responsibility?**

If no:

> **Do not share it.**

---

# 63. The Classification Test

Ask:

> **What is the information's current University security/privacy classification?**

If unknown and the information is sensitive:

> **Verify before sharing or moving it.**

---

# 64. The Minimum-Information Test

Ask:

> **What is the least amount of information needed to accomplish the legitimate purpose?**

Use that amount.

---

# 65. The Authorized-System Test

Ask:

> **Is this device, platform, repository, cloud service, or AI system approved for this information?**

If uncertain:

> **Do not upload or transfer it.**

---

# 66. The Record Test

Ask:

> **Is this an official University record, transitory working information, or something else?**

The answer affects storage, retention, and disposal.

---

# 67. The Authoritative-Source Test

Ask:

> **Where is the official version of this information?**

Do not let copies become mistaken for authoritative records.

---

# 68. The Retention Test

Ask:

> **How long must this record be kept under the current University retention schedule?**

Do not invent retention periods.

---

# 69. The Disposal Test

Ask:

> **Is this record currently eligible for destruction, and who has authority to approve disposal?**

If uncertain:

> **Do not destroy it.**

---

# 70. The Breach Test

Ask:

> **Has information been lost, exposed, accessed, transmitted, or used in an unauthorized way?**

If yes or reasonably possible:

> **Use the current University breach-response process immediately.**

---

# 71. The Investigation / Hold Test

Ask:

> **Could this information be relevant to a current or reasonably anticipated complaint, grievance, investigation, legal matter, audit, or incident?**

If yes:

> **Preserve it and obtain guidance before disposal.**

---

# 72. The AI Test

Before putting real case information into Claude, ask:

> **Would Mike be permitted to disclose this same information to this external system if it were not an AI?**

If the answer is no or uncertain:

> **Do not upload it.**

# 77. Common Errors Claude Shall Prevent

Claude should warn against:

- Treating all University information as public.
- Accessing employee information without legitimate need.
- Sharing information because someone is "part of management" without verifying need.
- Maintaining shadow personnel files.
- Storing sensitive records indefinitely.
- Destroying records because they are inconvenient.
- Deleting information during a grievance or investigation.
- Forwarding sensitive information to personal email.
- Using unauthorized personal cloud storage.
- Sharing credentials.
- Uploading sensitive University information to an AI system without authorization.
- Assuming de-identification automatically removes every privacy concern.
- Treating email or chat as outside records-management rules.
- Treating a technical ability to access data as authorization to use it.
- Ignoring misdirected emails or accidental exposure.
- Quietly concealing an information breach.
- Inventing retention periods.
- Treating repository copies as authoritative University records.
- Treating AI output as automatically suitable for an official personnel file.

# 82. Foundational Policy 46 Doctrine

The Mechanical Services Supervisor Intelligence System shall approach University information according to the following doctrine:

> **University information is an asset and a responsibility.**

> **Access does not equal ownership.**

> **Technical access does not equal authorization.**

> **Use information only for legitimate University purposes.**

> **Collect and share only what is reasonably necessary.**

> **Know the information classification before moving sensitive information.**

> **Use approved systems and controls.**

> **Protect confidential, restricted, and highly restricted information.**

> **Create accurate records close to the events they document.**

> **Document facts rather than character judgments.**

> **Know where the authoritative record lives.**

> **Do not create uncontrolled shadow personnel files.**

> **Follow current retention schedules.**

> **Do not destroy records because they are inconvenient.**

> **Preserve records relevant to disputes, investigations, incidents, or legal processes.**

> **Securely destroy information only when authorized.**

> **Report suspected information-security breaches promptly.**

> **Do not conceal accidental disclosure.**

> **Do not upload non-public University information into AI systems without authorization.**

> **Use de-identified or hypothetical scenarios when sensitive identity is unnecessary.**

> **The repository is an intelligence layer, not the University's official system of record.**

> **Verify current Policy 46 and supporting procedures before consequential information decisions.**

# 83. Final Policy 46 Statement

Policy 46 turns information management into an operational discipline.

For Mike, the important question is not simply:

> **Can I access this information?**

The better questions are:

> **Why do I need it?**

> **Am I authorized to use it?**

> **How sensitive is it?**

> **Where should it be stored?**

> **Who actually needs to see it?**

> **What is the official record?**

> **How long must it be kept?**

> **When and how can it be destroyed?**

> **Has anything been exposed or mishandled?**

> **Can this information safely and lawfully be entered into an AI system?**

Good supervision requires good information.

Good information requires:

> **accuracy,**

> **privacy,**

> **security,**

> **accountability,**

> **controlled access,**

> **proper retention,**

> **and responsible disposal.**

That is information becoming architecture.

That is the Policy 46 framework for the Mechanical Services Supervisor Intelligence System.
