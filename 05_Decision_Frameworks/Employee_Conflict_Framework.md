# 1. Purpose

This document provides Claude with a structured framework for supporting
Mike when workplace conflicts arise between employees, teams, or
stakeholders.

It is designed to help with:

-   Interpersonal disagreements.
-   Communication breakdowns.
-   Team tension.
-   Misunderstandings.
-   Collaboration challenges.
-   Restoring working relationships.
-   Determining when escalation is required.

This framework does not replace formal investigations, Human Resources
processes, Labour Relations guidance, collective agreement requirements,
or University policies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **A supervisor's role is not to decide who wins a conflict. It is to
> help restore a respectful, functional, and safe working
> relationship.**

Conflict is not always a failure.

Handled properly, conflict can reveal:

-   Communication gaps.
-   Process problems.
-   Unclear expectations.
-   Opportunities for improvement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. First Question

Before responding, ask:

> **What type of conflict is this?**

Possible categories:

-   Miscommunication.
-   Difference of opinion.
-   Work-style conflict.
-   Performance concern.
-   Safety concern.
-   Harassment or discrimination concern.
-   Behaviour issue.

The response should match the situation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Conflict Decision Sequence

``` text
Conflict identified
        |
        ▼
Determine immediate risk
        |
        ▼
Understand perspectives
        |
        ▼
Separate facts from emotions
        |
        ▼
Identify the real issue
        |
        ▼
Clarify expectations
        |
        ▼
Support resolution
        |
        ▼
Follow up
        |
        ▼
Escalate if required
```

# 5. Immediate Safety Assessment

First ask:

-   Is anyone threatened?
-   Is there harassment?
-   Is there violence?
-   Is there discrimination?
-   Is work safety affected?

If yes, use the appropriate framework.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Listen to Understand

Mike should:

-   Hear each person's perspective.
-   Avoid interrupting.
-   Ask clarifying questions.
-   Remain neutral.
-   Avoid making early judgments.

Listening does not mean agreeing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Separate Facts From Perceptions

Conflicts often contain both:

## Facts

Observable events.

Example:

"Employee A did not provide the requested information."

## Perceptions

Interpretations.

Example:

"Employee A does not respect me."

Both matter, but they are different.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Identify the Root Issue

Common causes:

## Communication Gap

People understood expectations differently.

Response: - Clarify. - Improve communication.

## Process Problem

The system creates unnecessary conflict.

Response: - Improve workflow.

## Expectation Problem

Roles or responsibilities are unclear.

Response: - Define expectations.

## Relationship Problem

Trust or respect has declined.

Response: - Facilitate conversation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Supervisor as Facilitator

Mike should avoid becoming:

-   The referee deciding who is right.
-   The messenger carrying complaints back and forth.
-   The person who solves every interpersonal issue.

Instead:

Help people:

-   Understand each other.
-   Clarify expectations.
-   Work toward a solution.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Respectful Conversation Model

A productive conversation:

## Describe

What happened.

## Explain

Why it matters.

## Listen

Understand the other perspective.

## Align

Agree on expectations.

## Commit

Determine next steps.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Communication Standards

Encourage:

-   Specific examples.
-   Respectful language.
-   Direct communication.
-   Problem-solving focus.

Avoid:

-   Personal attacks.
-   Gossip.
-   Assumptions about motives.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Conflict and Accountability

Respect does not mean avoiding accountability.

A supervisor can:

-   Listen compassionately.
-   Understand circumstances.
-   Still maintain standards.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Mechanical Services Examples

Potential conflicts:

-   Work assignment disagreements.
-   Scheduling concerns.
-   Different approaches to maintenance tasks.
-   Communication issues between trades.
-   Contractor/team misunderstandings.
-   Perceived unfairness.

# 14. Conflict Versus Harassment

Claude should help distinguish:

## Normal Workplace Conflict

-   Disagreement.
-   Different opinions.
-   Frustration.
-   Miscommunication.

## Harassment Concern

-   Prohibited behaviour.
-   Repeated harmful conduct.
-   Discrimination.
-   Threats.
-   Serious disrespect.

Use:

`Harassment_or_Discrimination_Framework.md`

when appropriate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Documentation

Document when appropriate:

-   Significant discussions.
-   Expectations clarified.
-   Agreements reached.
-   Follow-up actions.

Avoid documenting:

-   Personal opinions.
-   Emotional judgments.
-   Unverified claims.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Escalation Triggers

Mike should seek guidance when:

-   Conflict becomes harassment.
-   Safety is involved.
-   Conflict affects operations significantly.
-   Employees cannot resolve issues.
-   Formal processes may be required.
-   Collective agreement issues arise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Common Decision Traps

Claude should prevent:

## Taking Sides Too Quickly

Understand before deciding.

## Avoiding Conflict

Ignoring issues allows them to grow.

## Treating Every Conflict as Misconduct

Not every disagreement requires discipline.

## Becoming the Permanent Mediator

Build employee problem-solving ability.

## Focusing on Personalities

Focus on behaviours and outcomes.

# 19. Foundational Conflict Doctrine

> **Conflict is information.**

> **Listen before judging.**

> **Respect does not require agreement.**

> **Strong relationships require honest conversations.**

> **The goal is resolution, not victory.**

> **A supervisor creates the conditions where people can work together
> effectively.**

> **Healthy conflict handled well improves the organization.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. Final Statement

Employee conflict is one of the everyday tests of leadership.

Mike's questions should be:

> **What is really happening?**

> **What does each person need to be understood?**

> **What expectations need clarification?**

> **Is this conflict or something more serious?**

> **How can we restore a productive relationship?**

> **What can we learn from this situation?**

A strong workplace is not one without conflict.

It is one where conflict is handled with maturity, respect, and
accountability.

That is collaboration becoming architecture.
