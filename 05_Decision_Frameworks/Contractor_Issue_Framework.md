# 1. Purpose

This document provides Claude with a structured framework for supporting
Mike when issues arise involving contractors, vendors, or external
service providers.

It is designed to help with:

-   Contractor safety concerns.
-   Quality issues.
-   Communication problems.
-   Scope disagreements.
-   Equipment damage.
-   Procedure violations.
-   Coordination challenges.
-   Escalation decisions.

This framework does not replace University procurement requirements,
contracts, Safety Office procedures, legal requirements, or authorized
University processes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **Contractors are part of the workplace safety system. They must be
> managed with the same commitment to safety, respect, and
> accountability as internal teams.**

A contractor issue is not simply:

> "The contractor's problem."

The University environment and its people are still affected.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Contractor Decision Sequence

``` text
Issue identified
        |
        ▼
Is anyone unsafe?
        |
        ▼
Stabilize immediate risk
        |
        ▼
Understand facts
        |
        ▼
Review requirements and expectations
        |
        ▼
Communicate with responsible parties
        |
        ▼
Determine corrective action
        |
        ▼
Document and follow up
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Step One --- Assess Immediate Risk

Ask:

-   Is anyone exposed to danger?
-   Is equipment damaged?
-   Is campus operation affected?
-   Does work need to stop?

Safety concerns take priority over schedule.

Use:

`Immediate_Safety_Event_Framework.md`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Step Two --- Understand the Situation

Gather:

-   What happened?
-   Who was involved?
-   What work was being performed?
-   What expectations existed?
-   What documentation exists?
-   Was a procedure or requirement missed?

Separate facts from assumptions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Contractor Responsibilities

Contractors are expected to:

-   Follow applicable safety requirements.
-   Follow University procedures.
-   Perform work according to agreed expectations.
-   Communicate issues appropriately.
-   Correct deficiencies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. University Responsibilities

Plant Operations must:

-   Communicate expectations clearly.
-   Coordinate work effectively.
-   Identify hazards.
-   Provide necessary information.
-   Monitor compliance.

A successful contractor relationship requires both sides.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Safety and Contractor Work

Contractors must not be allowed to create unsafe conditions.

Consider:

-   Hazard assessments.
-   Required PPE.
-   Lockout requirements.
-   Work permits.
-   Access controls.
-   Communication requirements.

Use:

`HSEMS.md`

and relevant safety frameworks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Quality Concerns

When work quality is an issue, assess:

-   Was the scope clear?
-   Were standards communicated?
-   Was the work inspected?
-   Was the deficiency identified?
-   What correction is required?

Focus on resolving the issue, not creating unnecessary conflict.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Equipment Damage

If a contractor damages equipment:

Consider:

-   Protecting people first.
-   Stabilizing the situation.
-   Documenting conditions.
-   Determining facts.
-   Notifying appropriate stakeholders.
-   Following contractual processes.

Avoid immediate assumptions about fault.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Communication Framework

Strong contractor communication includes:

-   Clear expectations.
-   Respectful discussions.
-   Documented decisions.
-   Defined responsibilities.
-   Timely follow-up.

Poor communication creates preventable problems.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Contractor Safety Violations

If a contractor violates safety requirements:

Response may include:

-   Stop unsafe activity.
-   Correct immediate hazard.
-   Document concern.
-   Notify appropriate contacts.
-   Review future controls.

Safety expectations apply regardless of employer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Scope Disagreements

When contractor and University expectations differ:

Review:

-   Contract scope.
-   Work request.
-   Technical requirements.
-   Communication history.
-   Responsibilities.

Clarify before escalating.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Escalation Triggers

Mike should seek guidance when:

-   Serious safety concerns exist.
-   Contract interpretation is required.
-   Significant damage occurs.
-   Work cannot proceed.
-   Repeated deficiencies occur.
-   Procurement or legal issues arise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Incident Connection

Contractor incidents should be reviewed using:

`Incident_Investigation_Framework.md`

The goal:

-   Understand what happened.
-   Improve controls.
-   Prevent recurrence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Documentation

Record:

-   Date.
-   Contractor involved.
-   Work activity.
-   Concern identified.
-   Communication.
-   Actions taken.
-   Follow-up requirements.

Good documentation protects relationships and accountability.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Common Decision Traps

Claude should prevent:

## Assuming Contractor Means Less Responsibility

Contractors are part of the workplace system.

## Focusing Only on Blame

Facts and correction come first.

## Ignoring Safety Because They Are External

Safety expectations remain.

## Poor Documentation

Informal conversations may become unclear later.

## Allowing Repeated Issues

Trends require action.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Claude Response Format

For contractor issues, Claude should provide:

## Situation Summary

What is occurring.

## Immediate Risk

Whether safety is affected.

## Known Facts

Verified information.

## Requirements

Policies, procedures, contracts, or standards involved.

## Options

Possible responses.

## Recommended Path

Appropriate next step.

## Escalation

Who should be involved.

## Documentation

What should be recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Foundational Contractor Doctrine

> **A contractor is not outside the safety system.**

> **Clear expectations prevent conflict.**

> **Respectful communication improves accountability.**

> **Safety concerns are addressed regardless of who performs the work.**

> **Good contractor management protects people, operations, and
> relationships.**

> **Partnership requires clarity and accountability.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. Final Statement

Contractor management is a test of supervisory coordination.

Mike's questions should be:

> **Is everyone safe?**

> **What are the facts?**

> **What expectations apply?**

> **Who owns the next action?**

> **Who needs to be involved?**

> **How do we prevent this issue from returning?**

A strong contractor relationship is built through safety, communication,
and accountability.

That is collaboration becoming architecture.
