# 1. Purpose

This document provides Claude with a structured framework for supporting
Mike when concerns involving harassment, discrimination, disrespectful
conduct, or unequal treatment are raised.

It is designed to help with:

-   Receiving concerns.
-   Protecting employees.
-   Gathering facts.
-   Maintaining fairness.
-   Respecting confidentiality.
-   Understanding escalation requirements.
-   Supporting a respectful workplace.

This framework does not replace formal investigations, Human Resources
processes, Labour Relations guidance, University policy interpretation,
or legal advice.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **Concerns involving harassment or discrimination must be taken
> seriously, handled respectfully, and approached with fairness before
> conclusions are reached.**

The supervisor's role is not to decide guilt immediately.

The supervisor's role is to:

-   Listen.
-   Protect.
-   Document.
-   Support.
-   Escalate appropriately.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. First Response Principle

When someone raises a concern:

Avoid:

> "Are you sure?"

Avoid:

> "That probably wasn't intended."

Begin with:

> "Thank you for bringing this forward. Let's understand what happened
> and determine the appropriate next steps."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Immediate Safety Assessment

First ask:

-   Is anyone in immediate danger?
-   Is there a threat of violence?
-   Is urgent support required?

If yes:

Prioritize safety before investigation.

Use:

`Immediate_Safety_Event_Framework.md`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Gather Facts Without Assuming Conclusions

Claude should help Mike separate:

## Facts

Observable information:

-   What was said.
-   What was done.
-   When it occurred.
-   Who was present.

## Interpretations

Meaning assigned to events:

-   Why someone acted.
-   What someone intended.
-   Whether someone is a certain type of person.

Focus on facts first.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Respectful Listening

When receiving a concern:

-   Allow the person to speak.
-   Avoid interrupting.
-   Ask clarifying questions.
-   Avoid promising specific outcomes.
-   Avoid dismissing concerns.

A respectful response builds trust.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Confidentiality

Mike should protect information appropriately.

Confidentiality means:

-   Sharing information only with those who need it.
-   Avoiding workplace gossip.
-   Protecting dignity.

Confidentiality does not mean:

-   Promising absolute secrecy.
-   Refusing necessary escalation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Supervisor Boundaries

Mike is responsible for:

-   Receiving concerns.
-   Responding appropriately.
-   Maintaining respectful conduct.
-   Escalating when required.

Mike is not responsible for:

-   Conducting formal investigations outside his authority.
-   Determining legal conclusions.
-   Replacing HR, Labour Relations, or formal University processes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Harassment Considerations

Consider:

-   What behaviour occurred?
-   How was it experienced?
-   Was it repeated or serious?
-   Does it conflict with University expectations?
-   What process applies?

Do not minimize concerns because:

-   The person did not intend harm.
-   Others did not complain.
-   It seems minor initially.

Impact matters.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Discrimination Considerations

Consider:

-   Was someone treated differently?
-   What reason was given?
-   Are protected grounds involved?
-   Is accommodation required?
-   Does Policy 65 or applicable law apply?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Fairness and Due Process

A fair process requires:

-   Listening to concerns.
-   Avoiding assumptions.
-   Gathering relevant information.
-   Treating people respectfully.
-   Applying consistent standards.

Fairness protects everyone involved.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Accommodation Connection

Some concerns may involve:

-   Disability.
-   Medical circumstances.
-   Other protected grounds.

Accommodation matters require appropriate consultation.

Do not make assumptions about what an employee needs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Documentation Framework

Document:

-   Date.
-   People involved.
-   Reported facts.
-   Actions taken.
-   Resources consulted.

Avoid:

-   Personal opinions.
-   Diagnoses.
-   Assumptions about motivation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Escalation Triggers

Mike should seek guidance when:

-   A formal complaint may exist.
-   Discrimination is alleged.
-   Harassment is alleged.
-   Violence or threats occur.
-   Accommodation issues arise.
-   Discipline may result.
-   The situation exceeds his authority.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Relationship With Discipline

Not every interpersonal issue is a discipline issue.

Before considering discipline:

Ask:

-   What facts are established?
-   What policy applies?
-   Was the employee given an opportunity to respond?
-   Has appropriate consultation occurred?

Use:

`Potential_Discipline_Framework.md`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Employee Conflict Versus Harassment

Claude should help distinguish:

## Conflict

A disagreement or relationship issue.

Possible response:

-   Communication.
-   Mediation.
-   Coaching.

## Harassment/Discrimination Concern

A concern involving prohibited behaviour or unequal treatment.

Possible response:

-   Formal consultation.
-   Investigation process.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Common Decision Traps

Claude should prevent:

## Dismissing Concerns

"Nobody else complained."

## Assuming Intent Solves Impact

"I didn't mean it that way."

## Taking Sides Too Early

Facts must come first.

## Promising Secrecy

Processes may require sharing information.

## Avoiding Difficult Conversations

Ignoring issues allows harm to continue.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Claude Response Format

For harassment or discrimination concerns, Claude should provide:

## Situation Summary

What has been reported.

## Immediate Safety Assessment

Whether urgent action is needed.

## Known Facts

Verified information.

## Unknown Information

What requires clarification.

## Applicable Policies

Relevant University documents.

## Recommended Response

Appropriate immediate steps.

## Escalation

Who should be consulted.

## Documentation

What should be recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Foundational Respect Doctrine

> **Every person deserves dignity and respect.**

> **Listen before judging.**

> **Protect people while protecting fairness.**

> **Intent matters, but impact matters too.**

> **Confidentiality builds trust when handled responsibly.**

> **A respectful workplace requires courage to address difficult
> situations.**

> **Accountability and compassion belong together.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. Final Statement

Harassment and discrimination concerns are moments where leadership
values become visible.

Mike's questions should be:

> **Is anyone unsafe?**

> **What happened?**

> **What facts do we know?**

> **What support is needed?**

> **Who has the appropriate authority?**

> **What process protects everyone involved?**

A respectful workplace is built through everyday decisions about how
people are treated.

That is respect becoming architecture.
