# 1. Purpose

This document provides Claude with a structured framework for supporting
Mike when urgent mechanical equipment failures or infrastructure
disruptions occur.

It is designed for situations involving:

-   Mechanical equipment failure.
-   Critical infrastructure disruption.
-   Building system failures.
-   Emergency maintenance response.
-   Service interruptions.
-   Equipment damage.
-   Escalated operational issues.

This framework does not replace emergency procedures, technical manuals,
engineering requirements, manufacturer instructions, or University
response protocols.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **When critical equipment fails, the supervisor's responsibility is to
> protect people, stabilize the situation, restore operations safely,
> and learn from the failure.**

The priority order:

1.  Protect people.
2.  Control hazards.
3.  Stabilize operations.
4.  Communicate.
5.  Restore service.
6.  Investigate.
7.  Prevent recurrence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Equipment Failure Decision Sequence

``` text
Failure occurs
        |
        ▼
Is anyone at risk?
        |
        ▼
Stabilize hazard
        |
        ▼
Assess operational impact
        |
        ▼
Mobilize resources
        |
        ▼
Communicate with stakeholders
        |
        ▼
Repair or restore safely
        |
        ▼
Review cause and prevention
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. Step One --- Protect People

Before restoring equipment, ask:

-   Is anyone injured?
-   Is there an active hazard?
-   Could additional harm occur?
-   Does the area need to be isolated?

Never allow urgency to override safety.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Step Two --- Stabilize the Situation

Possible actions:

-   Stop equipment.
-   Isolate affected areas.
-   Control energy sources.
-   Prevent additional damage.
-   Restrict access.

Use:

`Hazardous_Energy_Control_Lockout.md`

when energy isolation is required.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Step Three --- Assess Impact

Consider:

-   Building impact.
-   Campus operations.
-   Occupant safety.
-   Research requirements.
-   Residence impacts.
-   Environmental effects.
-   Service priorities.

Not every equipment failure has the same urgency.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Mechanical Services Examples

Potential failures:

-   Pump failure.
-   Boiler or heating issue.
-   Cooling system failure.
-   Water leak.
-   Piping failure.
-   Mechanical equipment breakdown.
-   Control system failure.
-   Loss of critical building service.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Gather Facts Quickly

Determine:

-   What failed?
-   When did it fail?
-   Where is it located?
-   What systems are affected?
-   What alarms or indicators exist?
-   What changed before failure?
-   What resources are available?

Avoid guessing under pressure.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Communication Framework

Identify:

Who needs information?

Potential stakeholders:

-   Manager, Mechanical Services.
-   Plant Operations leadership.
-   Trades staff.
-   Building occupants.
-   Other Facilities teams.
-   Contractors.
-   Safety Office when appropriate.

Communication should be:

-   Accurate.
-   Timely.
-   Clear.
-   Appropriate for the audience.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Resource Coordination

Consider:

-   Internal trades.
-   Specialized expertise.
-   Contractors.
-   Tools.
-   Materials.
-   Replacement parts.
-   Technical documentation.

Good coordination prevents wasted effort.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Safety During Repairs

Before work begins:

Confirm:

-   Hazards identified.
-   Required controls in place.
-   Workers understand the task.
-   Proper PPE available.
-   Energy control completed where required.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Temporary Solutions

Temporary fixes require caution.

Ask:

-   Is the solution safe?
-   How long can it remain?
-   Who owns follow-up?
-   Does it create another risk?
-   Does it need documentation?

Temporary should not silently become permanent.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Operational Pressure

During failures, pressure increases.

Claude should remind Mike:

> **The fastest repair is not always the safest repair.**

A rushed decision can create:

-   Injury.
-   Equipment damage.
-   Repeat failure.
-   Larger disruption.

# 15. Documentation

Record:

-   Time of failure.
-   Equipment affected.
-   Actions taken.
-   People involved.
-   Communications.
-   Repairs completed.
-   Remaining risks.

Good records support future prevention.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Investigation After Stabilization

Once operations are restored, ask:

-   Why did the equipment fail?
-   Was maintenance adequate?
-   Were warning signs present?
-   Did procedures work?
-   Can reliability improve?

Use:

`Incident_Investigation_Framework.md`

when appropriate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. Preventative Maintenance Connection

Failures may reveal opportunities:

-   Improved inspections.
-   Better maintenance intervals.
-   Updated procedures.
-   Asset management improvements.
-   Training needs.

A failure can become a reliability improvement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Common Decision Traps

Claude should prevent:

## Repair Before Assessing Risk

Restoration cannot come before safety.

## Communication Failure

People cannot respond effectively without information.

## Temporary Fix Becoming Permanent

Follow-up is required.

## Ignoring Root Cause

Restoring service is not the same as preventing recurrence.

## Working Outside Expertise

Know when to involve others.

# 20. Foundational Equipment Failure Doctrine

> **Protect people before protecting equipment.**

> **Stabilize first, troubleshoot second.**

> **Clear communication prevents chaos.**

> **Urgency requires discipline, not panic.**

> **Reliable systems come from learning from failures.**

> **The best repair is the one that prevents the next failure.**

> **Operational excellence and safety are connected.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 21. Final Statement

Equipment failures test a supervisor's ability to balance urgency,
safety, and leadership.

Mike's questions should be:

> **Is anyone at risk?**

> **What failed?**

> **What is the impact?**

> **What needs to happen now?**

> **Who needs to be involved?**

> **How do we prevent this from happening again?**

Mechanical reliability is not just about fixing equipment.

It is about protecting the people, buildings, and mission supported by
that equipment.

That is operational excellence becoming architecture.
