# 1. Purpose

This document provides Claude with a structured framework for supporting Mike when addressing employee performance concerns.

It is designed for situations involving:

- Work quality concerns.
- Missed expectations.
- Skill gaps.
- Productivity concerns.
- Communication issues.
- Reliability concerns.
- Training needs.
- Coaching conversations.
- Potential escalation.

This framework does not replace University employment policies, collective agreement requirements, Human Resources guidance, Labour Relations processes, or formal performance procedures.

---

# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **A performance concern is first an opportunity to understand, support, and improve before it becomes a disciplinary matter.**

A supervisor's goal is not simply to identify problems.

The goal is to help people succeed while maintaining operational standards.

---

# 3. Performance Versus Misconduct

Claude should help Mike distinguish:

## Performance Concern

A person may be unable to meet expectations.

Examples:

- Skill gap.
- Lack of training.
- Unclear expectations.
- Lack of resources.
- Need for coaching.

## Misconduct Concern

A person may choose not to meet expectations.

Examples:

- Refusal to follow instructions.
- Intentional disregard of procedures.
- Dishonesty.
- Serious behaviour concerns.

The response should match the situation.

---

# 4. The Performance Decision Sequence

```text
Concern identified
        |
        ▼
Clarify expectations
        |
        ▼
Gather facts
        |
        ▼
Determine cause
        |
        ▼
Provide support/coaching
        |
        ▼
Monitor improvement
        |
        ▼
Escalate if required
```

---

# 5. Step One — Confirm the Expectation

Before addressing performance, ask:

- Was the expectation clear?
- Was the employee aware?
- Was the standard reasonable?
- Was the procedure available?
- Was the employee trained?

A person cannot consistently meet an expectation they do not understand.

---

# 6. Step Two — Gather Facts

Avoid assumptions.

Ask:

- What happened?
- How often has it occurred?
- What impact did it have?
- Has feedback been provided before?
- Are there contributing factors?

Focus on observable behaviour and results.

---

# 7. Step Three — Identify the Root Cause

Possible causes:

## Knowledge Gap

The employee does not know what to do.

Response:
- Training.
- Demonstration.
- Clarification.

## Skill Gap

The employee understands but needs development.

Response:
- Coaching.
- Practice.
- Support.

## Resource Barrier

The employee lacks tools, time, or information.

Response:
- Remove barriers.

## Behaviour Choice

The employee understands expectations but chooses not to follow them.

Response:
- Address accountability.

---

# 8. Coaching Mindset

Effective coaching:

- Is timely.
- Is respectful.
- Uses specific examples.
- Explains impact.
- Creates a path forward.
- Includes follow-up.

Avoid:

- Vague criticism.
- Personal attacks.
- Public embarrassment.

---

# 9. The Coaching Conversation

A strong structure:

## Describe

What happened.

## Explain

Why it matters.

## Listen

Understand their perspective.

## Align

Clarify expectations.

## Support

Identify what is needed.

## Follow Up

Review progress.

Performance issues involving safety are different.

Ask:

- Was the hazard understood?
- Was training provided?
- Was the procedure followed?
- Was anyone placed at risk?

# 12. Fairness and Consistency

Mike should consider:

- Have expectations been applied consistently?
- Have similar situations been handled similarly?
- Has the employee had opportunity to improve?
- Are there relevant circumstances?

Fairness builds trust.

---

# 13. Documentation

Document:

- Expectations communicated.
- Coaching provided.
- Support offered.
- Follow-up dates.
- Improvement observed.

Documentation should tell the story of reasonable supervision.

---

# 14. Escalation Toward Discipline

Performance concerns may eventually require formal processes.

Before escalation, consider:

- Clear expectations.
- Previous coaching.
- Training provided.
- Opportunity to improve.
- Consistency.
- Collective agreement requirements.

`Potential_Discipline_Framework.md`

# 15. Supervisor Authority

Mike can:

- Set expectations.
- Provide feedback.
- Assign work.
- Coach employees.
- Identify development needs.

Mike should consult when:

- Formal discipline is considered.
- Collective agreement rights may apply.
- The situation is complex.
- Previous corrective action exists.

---

# 16. Common Decision Traps

Claude should prevent:

## Jumping to Discipline

Not every issue is misconduct.

## Ignoring Problems

Avoiding difficult conversations allows issues to grow.

## Making It Personal

Address behaviour and outcomes, not character.

## Inconsistent Standards

Different treatment damages trust.

## Assuming Training Solves Everything

Some issues require process or resource changes.

# 18. Foundational Performance Doctrine

> **People deserve the opportunity to succeed.**

> **Clear expectations create fair accountability.**

> **Coaching builds capability.**

> **Accountability requires consistency.**

> **A supervisor develops people, not just evaluates them.**

> **Correcting a problem is an act of respect when done properly.**

> **The goal is improved performance and a stronger team.**

---

# 19. Final Statement

Performance management is one of the clearest examples of values becoming action.

Mike's questions should be:

> **Was the expectation clear?**

> **Does the employee understand what is needed?**

> **What is preventing success?**

> **What support can be provided?**

> **Has the employee had a fair opportunity to improve?**

> **What response best serves the employee, the team, and the University?**

Good supervision creates conditions where people can do their best work.

That is accountability becoming architecture.
