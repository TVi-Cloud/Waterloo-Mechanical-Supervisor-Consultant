# 1. Purpose

This document provides Claude with a structured framework for supporting
Mike when an employee refuses work because they believe it is unsafe.

This situation requires careful balance between:

-   Worker safety rights.
-   Supervisor responsibilities.
-   Operational requirements.
-   Collective agreement considerations.
-   Fair investigation.
-   Respectful communication.

This framework does not replace applicable legislation, University
procedures, Safety Office guidance, Labour Relations advice, or formal
processes.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Principle

The foundational rule:

> **A safety concern is a signal to investigate, not an inconvenience to
> dismiss.**

A supervisor's first responsibility is not proving someone wrong.

It is understanding the concern and ensuring the work can proceed
safely.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. The Wrong Responses

Claude should prevent these reactions:

## Immediate Discipline

"Employee refused work, therefore discipline is required."

Incorrect because the reason for refusal must be understood.

## Immediate Dismissal

"They are just avoiding work."

Incorrect because safety concerns may reveal real hazards.

## Automatic Acceptance Without Assessment

"The worker refused, so nobody works."

Incorrect because the concern still requires evaluation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. The Correct Decision Sequence

``` text
Worker raises safety concern
        |
        ▼
Listen and understand concern
        |
        ▼
Determine immediate risk
        |
        ▼
Assess hazard and facts
        |
        ▼
Apply controls
        |
        ▼
Consult if required
        |
        ▼
Determine appropriate next step
        |
        ▼
Document and follow up
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Step One --- Listen First

The supervisor should:

-   Allow the worker to explain the concern.
-   Ask questions.
-   Avoid becoming defensive.
-   Thank the worker for raising the issue.

A worker reporting a hazard is participating in safety.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Step Two --- Understand the Concern

Ask:

-   What specifically feels unsafe?
-   What hazard is present?
-   Has something changed?
-   Has this happened before?
-   Is there a procedure?
-   Was training provided?
-   What control is missing?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Step Three --- Determine Immediate Risk

Ask:

> "Is someone currently exposed to serious danger?"

If yes:

Prioritize:

1.  Stop or control the hazard.
2.  Protect workers.
3.  Obtain assistance.
4.  Investigate afterward.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Step Four --- Assess the Hazard

Consider:

-   Equipment condition.
-   Procedure requirements.
-   Training.
-   Environmental conditions.
-   Required PPE.
-   Energy sources.
-   Previous incidents.
-   Applicable regulations.

Use:

`Hazardous_Energy_Control_Lockout.md`

when energy hazards are involved.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Step Five --- Determine Controls

Possible outcomes:

## Hazard Confirmed

Correct the hazard before proceeding.

## Additional Controls Needed

Implement protections before continuing.

## Information Gap

Gather more information.

## Concern Not Supported

Explain findings respectfully and document the process.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Communication Framework

Avoid:

> "You have to do it."

Use:

> "Help me understand the safety concern so we can determine the right
> way forward."

Good communication protects both safety and working relationships.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Documentation

Record:

-   Date and time.
-   Task involved.
-   Concern raised.
-   Facts gathered.
-   Actions taken.
-   Controls implemented.
-   People consulted.

Document facts, not assumptions about motivation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Collective Agreement Considerations

For CUPE Local 793 employees:

Consider:

-   Employee rights.
-   Grievance rights.
-   Representation.
-   Established procedures.

Claude should reference:

`CUPE_793_Grievance_Procedure.md`

and applicable collective agreement sections.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Discipline Considerations

A refusal related to a genuine safety concern should not automatically
become a discipline matter.

Before considering discipline, assess:

-   Was there a legitimate safety concern?
-   Were procedures followed?
-   Was the employee acting in good faith?
-   Were expectations clear?
-   Was the employee trained?

Use:

`Potential_Discipline_Framework.md`

when appropriate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Supervisor Authority

Mike can:

-   Listen.
-   Assess.
-   Stop unsafe work.
-   Correct hazards.
-   Assign appropriate work.
-   Escalate concerns.

Mike should consult when:

-   Rights are disputed.
-   Discipline is considered.
-   Legal interpretation is required.
-   The situation is complex.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Common Safety Culture Test

Ask:

> "If another employee saw how this situation was handled, would they
> feel comfortable reporting a hazard?"

A strong safety culture encourages reporting.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Common Errors Claude Shall Prevent

Claude should warn against:

-   Treating every refusal as insubordination.
-   Ignoring safety concerns.
-   Arguing before understanding.
-   Assuming bad intent.
-   Failing to document.
-   Allowing unsafe work to continue.
-   Escalating unnecessarily before facts are known.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

For unsafe work refusal situations, Claude should provide:

## Situation Summary

What is occurring.

## Safety Concern

What hazard is being reported.

## Immediate Risk

Whether work should stop.

## Information Needed

What facts are missing.

## Applicable Documents

Policies, procedures, and agreements.

## Recommended Approach

## Escalation

## Documentation

What should be recorded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Foundational Unsafe Work Doctrine

> **A worker who raises a safety concern is giving the system an
> opportunity to improve.**

> **Safety concerns deserve investigation before judgment.**

> **Respectful communication protects both workers and supervisors.**

> **The goal is safe work, not winning an argument.**

> **Accountability requires understanding facts before consequences.**

> **A supervisor's response to a safety concern defines the safety
> culture.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Final Statement

Unsafe work refusals are moments where safety, leadership, and trust
intersect.

Mike's questions should be:

> **What hazard is being identified?**

> **Is anyone at risk right now?**

> **What facts do we know?**

> **What controls are needed?**

> **Who needs to be involved?**

> **How do we resolve this while protecting people and maintaining
> trust?**

Safety becomes architecture when people know they can speak up and the
system responds responsibly.
