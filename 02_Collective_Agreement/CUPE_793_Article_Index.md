This document is the primary navigation index for the CUPE Local 793 Collective Agreement within the Mechanical Services Supervisor Intelligence System.

- Which Article is likely relevant.
- Which related Articles should also be reviewed.
- Whether a Letter of Understanding may apply.

- Which focused Collective Agreement topic file should be consulted.

This document is a navigation tool.

# 3. Mandatory Retrieval Rule

This index shall never substitute for retrieval of the actual agreement language.

When an Article appears relevant, Claude shall:

1. Identify the likely Article using this index.
2. Retrieve the Article from the Project Knowledge PDF.
3. Read the relevant clause or subsection.
4. Read surrounding language where context matters.
5. Check related Articles.
6. Check applicable Letters of Understanding.
7. Verify the current official University source.
8. Only then interpret the provision.

The governing rule is:

> **Index → Retrieve → Cross-reference → Verify → Interpret → Cite**

# 5. Supervisor Relevance Scale

This index uses the following retrieval priority system.

## CRITICAL

Claude should routinely consider these provisions.

## HIGH

Frequently relevant to supervision, staffing, employee relations, scheduling, safety, or work coordination.

## MODERATE

Relevant in particular circumstances but not necessarily part of daily supervisory decision-making.

## SPECIALIZED

Applies primarily to specific employee groups, programs, processes, or unusual situations.

The relevance rating is project-generated.

It does not indicate legal importance.

# 7. Article 1 — Recognition and Scope

**Supervisor Relevance:** CRITICAL

## Primary Issues

Article 1 should be retrieved when a situation involves:

- Bargaining-unit scope.
- Work normally performed by bargaining-unit employees.
- Supervisors performing bargaining-unit work.
- Emergency work.
- Staffing emergencies.
- Employee classification within the bargaining unit.
- Scope of CUPE representation.

## Why Mike Should Care

This Article directly affects the boundary between supervisory responsibility and bargaining-unit work.

A supervisor's ability to:

- Demonstrate work.
- Train.
- Assist.
- Respond during emergencies.
- Personally perform bargaining-unit work.

may be constrained by the agreement.

## Retrieval Triggers

Search Article 1 when Mike asks questions such as:

- "Can I do this repair myself?"
- "Can a supervisor help the trades staff?"
- "We're short staffed. Can I perform the work?"
- "Does this employee fall under CUPE?"
- "Can management assign this work outside the bargaining unit?"

## Related Authority

Potentially review:

- Article 2 — Management's Rights and Reservations.
- Article 7 — Representation.
- Article 11 — Hours of Work and Overtime.
- Article 25 — Health, Safety and Environment.

# 66. Underlying-Issue Rule

For grievances, discipline, or disputes, Claude should identify:

> **What underlying substantive issue created the dispute?**

- Article 16 for procedure.

# 67. Procedure Versus Substance

Claude shall distinguish:

### Substantive Provision

What rights, obligations, or rules apply?

from:

### Procedural Provision

How is disagreement handled?

Example:

- Article 11 may establish the overtime rule.
- Article 16 may establish how a dispute over that rule is grieved.

Both may be necessary.
